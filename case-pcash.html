Case study · Brand, UX, front-end

P.Cash — the site that sells me. You're inside it right now

This isn't a description of the project — it is the project. Everything described below is running on the page you're reading: the background, the type, the section transitions, the accessibility work. Every claim can be checked without leaving the tab.

Role: brand · UX · front-end · copy Solo project Vanilla JS + GSAP 5 pages · RU/EN

The premise

Why a freelancer needs their own site

My site is the first thing a client judges me by, long before they see a line of my code. So it has to prove the skill rather than describe it. Anyone can write "I build fast, accessible sites"; only the page itself can prove it.

Hence the rule every effect was judged by: it stays only if it helps convey the content or demonstrates a specific skill. Everything that was merely "nice" was cut — and below you can see exactly what, and why.

Decisions

Seven decisions, each with a reason

None of them are a matter of taste. Each has a constraint it grew out of, and a verifiable payoff.

01 · One colour, one role

Constraint

The palette has three accents: orange, violet and green. Without strict roles they bleed: orange ends up on the CTA button, the price tag, and the heading — the eye can't tell which one is an action.

Decision

Each accent is locked to one role. Orange — CTAs and prices (with dark text for WCAG AA contrast). Violet — interactive elements and highlights. Green — status indicators (online, ready) and focus rings. The CSS variable is --accent, never a colour name.

Payoff

The rule is learned once and holds site-wide: orange — act, violet — interact, green — status. And dark text on orange passes at 11.2:1 contrast ratio.

02 · The section counter became navigation

Constraint

A "01 / 11" indicator sat in the corner. It showed a number — and nothing else.

Decision

Replaced with a column of dots: each one anchors to a section, the active one is highlighted, and hovering names it.

Payoff

The same screen real estate now gives navigation across a long page instead of decoration.

03 · One particle system instead of two

Constraint

A layer of dots ran on top of the background shader. Two particle systems at once make the background noisy and pull attention off the copy.

Decision

The dots were replaced by ten schematic symbols — one per thing I sell: microchip, terminal, neural net, API, database, automation, artboard, Bézier curve, git branch, waveform.

Payoff

Same canvas, same render loop — but the background stopped competing with the copy and started speaking about the content.

04 · The 3D card tilt was removed

Constraint

Service cards tilted toward the cursor — striking in a demo, but alien next to the flat, calm block layout.

Decision

Removed outright. In its place: a 2–4px lift and a violet border highlight — the same feedback, but in the logic of flat surfaces.

Payoff

Hover feedback became uniform across every card on the site — the blocks stopped looking like different systems.

05 · Budget calculator → price teaser

Constraint

The homepage had an interactive calculator: tick services, get a range. It asked the visitor to do work before they understood the ballpark.

Decision

Replaced with a short teaser: four starting prices, the payment terms in one line, and a link to a dedicated pricing page with scope.

Payoff

The ballpark reads in a couple of seconds; the detail stays available for those who actually need it.

06 · Surfaces split into two classes

Constraint

A living background under the whole page looks good, but reading text over a moving gradient is hard.

Decision

Surfaces were split by role: those carrying the main copy are solid dark; secondary ones stay translucent so the background reads through. Sections themselves are transparent, and the separators between them were removed.

Payoff

The copy stays readable, the background stays visible, and the page scrolls as one seamless canvas.

07 · Editorial serif for display type

Constraint

The original headings used the same sans-serif as the body. The page had no typographic identity — nothing to distinguish the portfolio from a generic developer template.

Decision

Cormorant Garamond — a high-contrast Garamond reinterpretation with full Latin and Cyrillic support — for all display headings. Weight 600 for readability on dark backgrounds. Body text stays on Work Sans.

Payoff

The tension between an editorial serif and a clean geometric sans mirrors the brand: creative vision executed with engineering discipline. The hairline serifs catch light against the dark void.

Performance

A background three times cheaper, and it doesn't show

The background is neither an image nor a video but a WebGL shader: fractal noise warped by a second one, producing soft cloud banks with no hard edges. An effect like this easily becomes a battery drain, so it carries three decisions that each save work while staying invisible.

  • Three noise octaves instead of fiveThe last two produce detail finer than a visible wisp of fog — literally indistinguishable. They cost about 40% of the shader's work.
  • Rendering at 45% resolutionFog is nothing but smooth gradients, so it doesn't need one texel per screen pixel. Rendering at 45% and letting the GPU upscale costs roughly five times fewer fragment shader runs.
  • 30 frames per second instead of 60The fog drifts at 0.035 units per second. At that speed 30fps is indistinguishable from 60, and it halves the GPU's work.
  • And a full shutdown where the effect isn't wantedRendering pauses on a hidden tab, switches off on touch devices, and shuts down entirely under a reduced-motion preference, falling back to a static CSS gradient.

Accessibility

What a screenshot can't show

A dark theme with a moving background is the easiest way to quietly ruin accessibility. So here it was built into the same decisions rather than bolted on at the end.

  • Contrast holds at AAA, not the AA minimumBody copy on dark backgrounds stays above 7:1 — WCAG's AAA level. Orange buttons use dark text (noir) instead of white, because white on orange only reaches 2.6:1 — below even the AA threshold. The dark text passes at 11.2:1.
  • Reduced motion means off, not slower30 separate prefers-reduced-motion blocks in the styles and 6 guards in the script. The shader never starts, animations collapse to instant, inertia scrolling yields to native. The user gets the same content and the same hierarchy — without the motion.
  • Section reveals run on IntersectionObserver, not scroll eventsLenis drives the scrolling, and one missed event once left an entire section permanently invisible. A safety net was added on top: anything still hidden after load is revealed outright. Content cannot be lost to an animation glitch.
  • The small things usually forgottenA skip-link first in tab order. The burger's aria-expanded is genuinely updated — a static "false" lies to screen readers. A visible focus ring on every interactive element. On print, the dark canvas gives way to plain text.

Being upfront

What's deliberately absent

The biggest risk for a beginner's portfolio is looking more experienced than you are. Three places where lying would have been easier were resolved the other way.

  • The reviews section was removed entirelyAlong with its submission form. There are no reviews yet, and an empty block with a form implies they exist somewhere — a quiet lie. When real ones arrive, the section comes back.
  • "Work" shows self-initiated projects, no client logosIt's stated plainly in the section subtitle and inside every case study. Logos of companies I haven't worked with would borrow trust and destroy it at the first question.
  • Analytics is wired up but switched offThe Plausible setup — no cookies, no personal data — sits commented out and switches on with a single line. Until there's a domain, nothing is collected and nothing leaves the page.

Stack

What the site is built with

No framework. Every dependency was chosen because the vanilla alternative would have been measurably worse, not because it was more convenient.

  • HTML + CSS + Vanilla JSStatic files that work on any host. Zero build steps, zero configs, zero node_modules in production. Typography: Cormorant Garamond (display), Work Sans (body), Space Mono (labels).
  • GSAP + ScrollTriggerSection reveals, parallax and transitions. An IntersectionObserver fallback guarantees content is visible even if GSAP fails to load.
  • LenisInertia scrolling. Disabled entirely under prefers-reduced-motion — native scroll is better for users who don't want motion.
  • Three.js (background WebGL shader)Fractal noise rendered at 45% resolution at 30fps. On touch devices and under reduced-motion it falls back to a static CSS gradient.

Metrics

Numbers you can check

  • Lighthouse: 100 / 100 / 100 / 100Performance, Accessibility, Best Practices, SEO — on every page. Not a screenshot of a lucky run, but a stable result.
  • 30 prefers-reduced-motion blocks in CSS + 6 JS guardsEvery effect that could cause discomfort is disabled by a system preference. Content and hierarchy are fully preserved.
  • Contrast ≥ 7:1 (AAA) on all body textTwice as strict as the required 4.5:1. The dark cloud banks are tuned so contrast never dips below this threshold.
  • 5 pages · full RU/EN · 0 frameworksEverything is translated: headings, forms, hints, validation errors, bot copy. Not "main content in two languages" but every single string.

The upshot

What it adds up to

  • The site doesn't depend on a frameworkThese are plain static files: they can go on almost any host, and they won't break because someone else's library shipped a new major version.
  • Two languages in full, not halfEvery page is translated along with form labels, hints and the bot's replies — not just the headlines on the homepage.
  • Motion and contrast are checked, not eyeballedAll animation switches off with a system preference, and the dark theme's colours were checked against contrast requirements.

This isn't a list of achievements. It's what follows from every decision above growing out of a constraint rather than a preference.
